## Roof workflow

Choose **Roof** in Plan and drag between opposite corners of a rectangular footprint. Select the roof or use **Roof schedule → View and select roofs** to choose gable (two planes), skillion (one sloping plane), flat (one horizontal plane), or **Hip — four equal-pitch planes**. The example house starts with a gable roof. **Layers → Roofs** independently hides/shows all roofs in Plan and 3D without changing quantities or deleting the roof. Plan shows the footprint, ridge and fall directions; hip roofs also show four hip lines and four fall arrows. 3D shows roof planes and edge faces. Plan uses the active level; 3D obeys per-level visibility.

For gable/skillion roofs, width runs across the slope and length along the ridge in local roof coordinates. Hip roofs use local X width and Y length, with one pitch and eave elevation for all four faces. Their ridge follows the longer overhang-inclusive dimension; changing width/length can turn that ridge without changing the rotation field. Square footprints form a pyramidal hip with one apex. Rotation turns the complete footprint. Overhang is horizontal in plan and added on every edge. Low-eave elevation is the roof's low top surface in the project datum; schematic body depth extends below it. Flat form ignores stored pitch and uses 0°. The supported 0°–75° range is a geometry-tool limit, not construction permission.

Gross sloping surface is plan area / cos(pitch), including overhang without waste or overlap/opening deductions. Hip rise is half the shorter overhang-inclusive dimension × tan(pitch); ridge length is the longer minus shorter dimension. Hip envelope volume is plan area × body depth + rise × (plan area / 2 − shorter dimension² / 6). All envelope volumes include enclosed void and schematic body depth, so they are not material volumes. Roof schedule/CSV add ridge and total 3D hip lengths; CSV includes four hip endpoint pairs. These are geometry lengths, not capping order quantities. Separate roofs remain independent until explicit trimming is enabled in a junction record. Framing members, gutters and product quantities are not generated from roof shape.

Roof design inputs record cladding/product, manufacturer minimum pitch, wind classification/design, framing/trusses, tie-down, drainage/overflow, sarking/condensation/ventilation, drawing revision and adopted NCC/standard editions. Missing records and pitch below the entered product minimum are flagged. Official basis: [ABCB Housing Provisions Section 7](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/system/files/ncc/abcb-housing-provisions-2022-20230501b.pdf), especially 7.2 (metal roofs), 7.3 (tiles/shingles, with hip/ridge fixing scope in 7.3.2 and flashing in 7.3.3), and 7.4 (gutters/downpipes), subject to project adoption and application limits. The hip/ridge and flashing clause scopes were verified against the official indexed source on 26 October 2026. Different end elevations produce a sloping envelope. The first outline edge and initial height are illustrative, not a compliant design. Members are edited through their parent deck; they are not separately selectable on canvas, do not automatically follow deck edits, and are not clipped or joined to deck edges or stairs.

The **Barriers** layer independently controls labelled plan lines and translucent schematic 3D panel envelopes. The fill does not specify glass, panels, posts, individual rails or openings. Export the barrier CSV from a selected deck for all project runs, geometry and design records. Takeoffs report sloping run length, not a material order or an assessment of complete edge protection. JSON save/open, autosave and undo/redo retain records; legacy projects start with no barriers.

### Measured stair details

Choose **Stair** in Plan and drag from the lower nosing centre towards the upper nosing centre. The run direction can be any plan angle. Edit width, endpoints, total finished rise, number of risers, lower finished elevation and schematic tread thickness. Starting dimensions are illustrative geometry. **Stair schedule → View and select stairs** accesses flights behind other layers or on other levels. **Layers → Stairs** independently shows/hides stairs in plan and 3D without changing schedules.

A flight with N risers has N−1 separate treads in this model; the upper floor or entered landing supplies the final surface. R = total rise / N; G = horizontal run / (N−1). The inspector and CSV report R, G and 2R+G along with run and upper elevation. Plan shows an UP arrow and riser lines; 3D shows tread plates and optional closed riser faces. Select a stair and choose an upper landing, straight continuation, quarter turn left/right or half turn left/right. Enter the landing length/width/thickness and, for a continuation, the second flight width/run/rise/risers; half turns also use an entered clear gap. Left/right is named while walking up in Plan. Landing top area and known body volume feed preliminary takeoff rows. Open-riser geometry requires a separate opening check. The updated example remains a straight flight ending at the deck's outer edge, with design/support details unspecified.

Project records cover material, stringers/supports/fixings, landings, headroom, handrails, barriers, slip resistance, drawing revision and applicable NCC edition/design criteria. The Australian review reference is the adopted project's [ABCB Housing Provisions Parts 11.2 and 11.3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/system/files/ncc/ncc2022-abcb-housing-provisions.pdf), including dimensional tables and application limits, slip resistance, landings, barriers and handrails. Confirm AS 4586 product-test evidence where applicable. These are reference/input prompts: no dimensional regulatory pass, structural sizing, headroom intersection, finished-site uniformity, landing connection or handrail/barrier compliance is inferred. The model supports one horizontal landing and at most one continuation flight per stair object; winders, curved/segmented turns, more than two flights, stringers, rails, barriers, final floor connections and structural interfaces are not generated. A continuation remains a preview until all required dimensions fit the entered landing; missing or non-fitting dimensions stay unassessed. The modelling limits (2–100 risers per flight, positive dimensions, 1,000,000 mm dimension and 1,000,000,000 mm absolute coordinate caps) are not regulatory permissions.
